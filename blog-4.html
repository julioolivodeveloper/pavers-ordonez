The Essential Paver Maintenance Guide for South Florida Homeowners

June 18, 2025 6 min read Pavers Ordonez Team

Florida's climate — intense UV radiation, heavy summer rains, high humidity, and occasional freezes in our northern counties — creates unique challenges for outdoor paver surfaces. The good news: properly maintained pavers can last 30–50 years. The bad news: neglected pavers can deteriorate in as few as 5–7 years. Here's everything you need to know to protect your investment.

Annual Maintenance Schedule

Think of paver maintenance like car maintenance: skip it for a year and you might be fine; skip it for five years and you're looking at a major repair bill. Here's the annual schedule we recommend for Palm Beach County homeowners:

  • January–February: Inspect for winter storm damage, re-level any shifted pavers, check joint sand levels
  • March–April: Deep clean with a paver-safe detergent and light pressure washing; apply fresh polymeric sand if joints are thin
  • May: Apply sealer (if on a 2-year schedule). This is the ideal pre-hurricane-season window
  • July–September: Monitor for weed growth; spot-treat with paver-safe herbicide
  • November: Post-storm inspection, clean debris, re-sand joints if needed

Sealing: The Most Important Step

Sealing is the single most impactful thing you can do for your pavers. A quality penetrating sealer does four things: locks joint sand in place, prevents weed germination, repels oil and stains, and enhances color. In South Florida's climate, we recommend sealing concrete pavers every 2–3 years and travertine every 1–2 years.

Sealer Types: Choose a penetrating sealer (not a topical film-forming sealer) for driveways. Film-forming sealers can peel in Florida's heat and leave your pavers looking worse than before. Ask us about the brands we use and trust.

Cleaning Your Pavers the Right Way

Regular cleaning removes the biological buildup — algae, mildew, dirt, and organic stains — that South Florida's humidity accelerates. Here's how to do it correctly:

  1. Blow off loose debris with a leaf blower first
  2. Apply a paver-safe cleaner (pH neutral for travertine, alkaline for concrete/brick) and let it dwell for 10–15 minutes
  3. Pressure wash at 1,200–1,500 PSI with a wide fan tip — never a zero-degree tip, which can damage the paver surface and blow out joint sand
  4. Rinse thoroughly with clean water
  5. Allow to dry completely (48 hours) before applying sealer

Never use bleach on pavers — it kills the surrounding grass and plants, can discolor certain paver materials, and provides only temporary results before biological growth returns faster than before.

Weed and Ant Control

Weeds and fire ants are the two most common complaints from Palm Beach County paver owners. Both are preventable with the right approach:

Weeds grow in the joints when sand is depleted. The solution is keeping joints fully packed with polymeric sand, which contains binding polymers that inhibit weed seed germination. If weeds appear, remove them manually and treat with a glyphosate-based herbicide, then re-sand and seal.

Ant colonies excavate joint sand from beneath, causing pavers to sink and become unstable. Treat immediately with fire ant bait granules around the perimeter and between joints. Then re-sand and compact the affected area.

When to Call a Professional

DIY maintenance works for routine cleaning and re-sanding. But call Pavers Ordonez when you see: sunken or rocking pavers (base failure), widespread efflorescence (white mineral deposits indicating moisture infiltration), cracked pavers (may indicate sub-base movement), or large weed-infested areas that require full joint cleaning and re-sanding.
