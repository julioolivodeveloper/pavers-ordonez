Top Landscaping Trends Transforming Palm Beach County Homes in 2025

May 10, 2025 5 min read Pavers Ordonez Team

Landscaping in South Florida has changed dramatically in the last few years. Rising water costs, stricter irrigation regulations, and growing environmental awareness have pushed homeowners toward smarter, more sustainable designs — without sacrificing beauty. Here are the trends we're seeing take hold across Palm Beach County in 2025.

1. Florida-Native Plants Front and Center

The biggest shift in residential landscaping across Palm Beach County is the move toward Florida-native plants. Plants like Coontie, Simpson's Stopper, Wild Coffee, Firebush, and Muhly Grass are showing up in more front and backyard designs than ever before — and for excellent reasons.

Native plants evolved in Florida's conditions and require little to no irrigation once established, resist local pests naturally, and support native wildlife including butterflies and birds. From a maintenance standpoint, they're dramatically easier to care for than exotic species that struggle in our climate. For homeowners looking to reduce their water bills and lawn care costs, a native plant transition is one of the smartest investments available.

2. Paver + Greenery Integration

One of the most visually striking design trends right now is the intentional blending of paver hardscaping with living greenery. Instead of the old approach — pavers on one side, lawn on the other — 2025 designs weave plantings into and around the paved surfaces.

Popular approaches include: planting beds between driveway strips, decorative ground cover between stepping stones, ornamental grasses bordering patio edges, and raised garden beds built directly into paver seating walls. This integration makes the outdoor space feel more organic and luxurious while reducing the total lawn area that needs to be mowed and irrigated.

Design Tip: Ground covers like Asiatic Jasmine or Mondo Grass between stepping stones look beautiful, suppress weeds naturally, and require no mowing. Ask us how to incorporate them into your paver design.

3. Low-Maintenance "Smart" Lawn Areas

Traditional high-maintenance St. Augustine lawns are giving way to smaller, strategically placed turf areas surrounded by mulch beds, river rock, and pavers. This "smart lawn" approach reduces water usage by 40–60% and cuts mowing frequency in half, while still giving kids and pets a green area to enjoy.

For the turf areas that remain, drought-tolerant varieties like Zoysia and Bahia are gaining popularity over St. Augustine for their lower water requirements and better resistance to Florida's dry winter periods.

4. Decorative Rock and Gravel Gardens

River rock, pea gravel, and decomposed granite are increasingly popular as a ground cover alternative in Palm Beach County. They're attractive, virtually maintenance-free, and excellent for drainage — a key consideration in our heavy-rain climate. Rock gardens also stay cooler than mulch beds and don't blow around in hurricanes the way mulch does.

When combined with drought-tolerant succulents, ornamental grasses, and specimen palms, a well-designed rock garden can be one of the most visually impactful and low-maintenance landscapes available.

5. Outdoor Living Rooms — Complete with Kitchens

The line between indoor and outdoor living continues to blur in South Florida. In 2025, the most requested outdoor feature is a fully equipped outdoor kitchen set into a paved entertaining area. Gas grills, undercounter refrigerators, concrete countertops, outdoor-rated cabinetry, and pergola shade structures are all part of the package.

These spaces start around $15,000 for a basic setup and can exceed $60,000 for full outdoor kitchen builds — but they dramatically increase both the enjoyment and the resale value of any home in Palm Beach County's year-round outdoor living climate.
