How Florida's Climate Affects Your Pavers (And What to Do About It)

August 5, 2025 5 min read Pavers Ordonez Team

West Palm Beach's climate is stunning — but it's also one of the harshest environments for outdoor hardscaping in the country. Between the intense UV exposure, heavy summer rainstorms, and occasional cold snaps, your pavers face serious stress year-round. Here's what you need to know to protect your investment.

The Rainy Season Problem

South Florida's rainy season (June through September) delivers over 30 inches of rainfall in just four months. When that water hits improperly installed or poorly jointed pavers, it erodes the base, causes settlement, and turns your driveway or patio into a tripping hazard. The fix? Proper drainage design from day one.

Every paver installation we do at Pavers Ordonez includes a 1/8" to 1/4" slope per linear foot to direct water away from structures. We also install a compacted base of 4–6 inches of crushed limestone — deep enough to handle South Florida's wet season without shifting.

UV Damage and Color Fading

Palm Beach County averages over 2,800 hours of sunshine per year. That constant UV exposure causes concrete pavers to fade noticeably within 3–5 years without protection. Sealing your pavers every 2–3 years is the single most effective way to maintain color vibrancy and prevent surface degradation.

Pro Tip: Choose a UV-resistant penetrating sealer rather than a surface coating. Penetrating sealers don't create a slippery film and won't peel or yellow over time the way topcoat sealers do in Florida's heat.

Weed and Algae Growth

Florida's humidity is a paradise for weeds and algae. They infiltrate joints, expand roots, and can cause individual pavers to shift. The solution is polymeric sand — a jointing material that hardens when wet, creating a firm barrier that resists weed germination and ant invasion. If you already have weed growth, we can pull and re-sand the affected areas during a maintenance visit.

Salt Air Near the Coast

If you're within a few miles of the Intracoastal or the ocean, salt air is an additional concern. It accelerates the breakdown of concrete-based materials and can stain travertine. For coastal properties, we recommend travertine or premium porcelain pavers, which are naturally more salt-resistant than standard concrete pavers.

What to Do Right Now

If your pavers are more than 3 years old and haven't been sealed, or if you're noticing joint sand erosion, pooling water, or sunken sections — call us for a free assessment. Catching problems early is always cheaper than a full reinstall.
